The free-rider on control

Why a raider who is the only bidder on earth still earns nothing — and what it takes to fix that.

A raider can lift the firm from v to v′ per share, at his own cost c. Every holder is small, so none of them decides whether the bid succeeds: each simply refuses unless the offer beats what a share is worth after the takeover. If the charter lets the winner take φ from anyone who did not sell, that floor drops to v′ − φ, and the raider keeps φ per share — plus whatever he appreciates on a stake α he bought quietly beforehand. So π = α(v′−v) + (1−α)φ − c, and he bids only if that is positive.

Two movements carry this lecture

Move one slider and watch a number move: that is comparative statics, and it is the whole method. These two are the ones worth remembering.

1 · He can only profit on what he owned beforehand
Betton, Eckbo & Thorburn (2009) estimate a toehold threshold averaging 9% across 10,000 control bids — toeholds are rare, and large where they occur.
2 · The fix cannot tell a good raider from a bad one
Value-destroying control deals do happen: Moeller, Schlingemann & Stulz (2005) find 87 of 4,136 acquisitions lost $397bn between them. That is a measurement of bidder shareholder losses, not of this dilution channel — a charter's φ is not something anyone observes in the data.
