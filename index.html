The 2026 course, week by week

A complete rebuild: Modigliani–Miller is the frictionless benchmark, and each week identifies one market friction, develops a compact model of it, and confronts the theory with the empirical evidence. Every week ships a lecture deck, a tutorial deck, and a hands-on Explorer.

A preview for colleagues — not the student-facing site (the student release runs through Brightspace from week 36). Everything here is self-contained: the PDFs open in the browser, and each Explorer is a single HTML file — move a slider, watch the answer move; that is the week’s comparative statics, felt rather than tabulated.

The assignment, updated

The multi-week replication project: teams of five reproduce the headline result of a published empirical corporate-finance paper from raw WRDS data, with AI use required, documented, audited and graded. Assessed at four moments (weeks 39, 41, 42, 43). For 2026–27 the weights move to 80% supervised exam / 20% project — the redesign, and the reasoning behind it, in full:

  • Week 36

    Frictions & the MM benchmark

    MM as the null — and how little the textbook frictions explain. The Aha: Miller’s personal-tax clawback. The tax gain to debt is not τcD, and its sign can flip.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: project kickoff Explorer: Miller’s clawback

    Deliverable: team enrolment + paper ranking.

  • Week 37

    Capital structure: dynamics & flexibility

    Borrowing today spends an option on borrowing tomorrow — lever up only if the tax shield beats the option you burn. Optimal under-leverage, derived.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: WRDS onboarding Explorer: debt capacity

    Papers + team-specific parameter sheets allocated.

  • Week 38

    External finance & the macro link

    A collateral limit turns a firm’s own net worth into a multiplier on what it can invest — and the micro friction aggregates into the macro accelerator.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: panel & regression Explorer: the multiplier

    Scoping memo due 22:00 Tue.

  • Week 39 M1 · scoping · 10 pts

    Incentives & compensation — the manager wedge

    Pay a manager more equity and he becomes more cautious. Convex pay buys the appetite back — then overshoots.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: M1 + wedge drill Explorer: the manager wedge

    M1 assessed & returned — the proceed / no-proceed gate. Mock exam posted.

  • Week 40

    Career Week

    No lecture, no tutorial. The mock exam is open for an optional attempt.

  • Week 41 M2 · reproduction · 25 pts

    The market for corporate control

    A raider who is the only bidder on earth still earns nothing — the target’s own shareholders free-ride the surplus away. So good takeovers are underprovided, and restoring them overshoots.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: event studies + M2 Explorer: the free-rider

    M2 assessed & returned — target cells vs the paper’s printed values.

  • Week 42 M3 · final package · 55 pts

    Risk management & financial regulation

    What is a euro in the bad state worth? To a constrained firm, more than one — so it hedges itself. To an insured bank, nothing — so it has to be made to. Capital is the deductible.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: M3 checklist Explorer: the taxpayer’s put

    M3 due end of week — reproducible repo, AI-process dossier, extension.

  • Week 43 M4 · defence · 10 pts

    Payout policy: smoothing as commitment

    The manager’s stake is collateral he never had to post — and a dividend is what happens when that stake reaches its ceiling. Smoothing as commitment, derived not assumed.

    Lecture deck Tutorial: defence day Explorer: the manager’s stake

    M4 — individual reflection + in-tutorial code defence.
