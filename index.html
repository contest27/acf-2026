Explorers

One per week, each a companion to that week’s lecture. Move a slider, watch the answer move — that is comparative statics, and it is the whole method.

Ungraded. Nothing here is handed in and none of it is examined directly. These exist so the mechanism becomes something you have felt rather than memorised. The exam asks you to do the same arithmetic by hand.
  • Week 36

    Miller’s clawback

    The tax gain to debt is not τcD. Personal taxes claw it back, and the sign can flip.

    Watch: the gain go negative, and Miller’s equilibrium where it is exactly zero.
  • Week 37

    Debt capacity as a real option

    Borrowing today spends an option on borrowing tomorrow. Lever up only if the tax shield beats the option you burn.

    Watch: optimal under-leverage appear when pG > τI.
  • Week 38

    Net worth as a multiplier

    A collateral limit turns a firm’s own net worth into a multiplier on what it can invest — and aggregates into the macro accelerator.

    Watch: the sensitivity jump from 1+λ to exactly zero. That step is Kaplan–Zingales’s objection.
  • Week 39

    The manager wedge

    Pay a manager more equity and he becomes more cautious. Convex pay buys the appetite back, then overshoots.

    Watch: his appetite peak and turn — the derivative crosses zero right where the lecture case sits.
  • Week 41

    The free-rider on control

    A raider who is the only bidder on earth still earns nothing, because the target’s own shareholders compete the surplus away.

    Watch: the stake he must already own before bidding pays — then push the charter until it starts paying raiders who destroy value.
  • Week 42

    The put the taxpayer wrote

    Insure the depositors and the bank stops caring how bad the bad state gets — so it pays to make it worse. Capital is the deductible.

    Watch: the flat premium change sign — the safe, well-capitalised bank ends up funding the reckless one.
  • Week 43

    Where the dividend starts

    He never posted his stake — he earned it. A dividend is what happens when it reaches its ceiling, and nobody puts that ceiling anywhere: it is solved for.

    Watch: make the manager harder to monitor and his buffer grows while the credit line shrinks — two measures of the same slack, moving opposite ways.
